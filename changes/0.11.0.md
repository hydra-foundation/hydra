Two lists of the same kind of thing can now share one sidebar entry. A module declared `->tabOf('jobs')` sits behind the Jobs entry instead of having its own, and every screen in the family shows a strip of tabs under its heading. The skeleton uses it for the queue (Jobs and Failed jobs) and the schedule (Scheduler and Runs), which takes the sidebar from twelve entries to ten. Settings draws its categories with the same strip. Slugs and URLs don't change, so bookmarks, links and audit rows land where they did.

This is 0.11.0 for two reasons. `Blueprint`'s constructor gains a `tabOf` argument before `ability`, so code that builds a `Blueprint` with positional arguments breaks. And the tab strip's classes are renamed, so an app's own copy of the skeleton's Settings strip loses its styling until it switches to the shared partial. Composer will not install this until you widen your constraint to `^0.11`.

## Upgrading

- **The admin:** `new Blueprint(...)` takes `tabOf` between `group` and `ability`. Pass it by name, or build a `Blueprint` with `Definition::compile()`, which is how modules get one.
- **The admin:** The strip is the shipped `admin/partials/tabs`, and its classes are
  `.admin-tabs` and `.admin-tab`. They replace `.settings-nav` and
  `.settings-tab`, which `admin.css` no longer styles: a view of your own that
  used them, such as a copy of the skeleton's `admin/settings/nav.php`, should
  render `admin/partials/tabs` instead.
- **The skeleton:** Copy `views/admin/settings/nav.php`. To take the new
  sidebar, add `->tabOf('jobs')` to `FailedJobsModule` and
  `->tabOf('scheduler')` to `ScheduledRunsModule`, and remove their `group()`
  and `icon()` lines. A tab that declares `group()` fails at boot.

## The admin

- A module can be a tab of another module with `->tabOf('jobs')`. It keeps its
  own slug, URL, source and screens, but gives up its own sidebar entry: the
  parent's entry stands for the family, stays highlighted on every tab, and leads
  to the first member the visitor may open. Every screen in the family shows a
  strip of tabs under its heading, leaving out any the gate denies. A tab of
  itself, a tab that also declares `group()`, a tab of a module that isn't
  registered and a tab of a tab all fail at boot (and in `admin:check`) with a
  message that names the fix.
