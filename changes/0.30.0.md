Hydra pages can be kept and revalidated. A controller says how a page may be kept and what it is made of, `HttpCache::public(maxAge: 300)->etag($post->slug, $mtime)`, and a reader who already holds it gets a `304` with no body. The ETag is built from those parts and the deploy, never from the HTML, which carries a fresh CSP nonce every time, so a deploy makes every page new. Pages that say nothing stay exactly as uncacheable as before, now with one `Cache-Control: no-store` instead of the three headers PHP's session used to stamp behind the app's back, and a public page that hands out a cookie is only ever kept by its reader. It is the third piece of the website's blog.

It is 0.30.0 because `http`, `core` and `kernel` gain API, and because responses change: an app has to add `HttpCacheMiddleware` to keep sending `no-store`. The skeleton also binds a `Release` from the new `APP_RELEASE` or the commit the checkout is on; copy that binding from its `AppServiceProvider` to have deploys change every ETag.

## Upgrading

- **HTTP:** Add `Hydra\Http\HttpCacheMiddleware` to your middleware stack, outside `StartSessionMiddleware` and `ErrorHandlerMiddleware` (the skeleton puts it just after `RequestLoggingMiddleware`). Without it your responses carry no `Cache-Control` at all, where they used to carry PHP's `no-store`.

## HTTP

- The native session no longer stamps `Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate`, `Expires: Thu, 19 Nov 1981` and `Pragma: no-cache` on every response. PHP's session cache limiter did that behind the app's back, so a controller that set its own `Cache-Control` sent two that disagreed. What a response may be kept for is now `HttpCacheMiddleware`'s to say, and it says `no-store` for anything that says nothing, as before.
- `HttpCacheMiddleware` handles every response's caching on its way out. A response that says nothing gets `Cache-Control: no-store`; a `public` one that sets a cookie, including the native session's, which PHP queues outside the PSR-7 response, is sent as `private`, so a shared cache never keeps someone's session; and a GET or HEAD 200 whose `ETag` or `Last-Modified` matches the request becomes a `304` with no body, so a controller that only calls `Responder::cached()` still saves its readers the download. No 304s while the `Release` is not conditional (development). Place it outside `StartSessionMiddleware` and `ErrorHandlerMiddleware`.
- A page can be kept and revalidated. `HttpCache::public(maxAge: 300)`, `HttpCache::private()` or `HttpCache::noStore()` says how a response may be kept; `->etag(...$parts)` and `->lastModified($at)` say what tells a later request it hasn't changed. The ETag is weak and hashed from the parts you give (a post's slug and modified time, say) plus the app's `Release`, never from the body, which carries a fresh CSP nonce every time; a deploy therefore makes every page new. `Responder::cached($response, $cache)` sets the headers, `Responder::isFresh($request, $cache)` says whether the reader already holds the page (RFC 9110: `If-None-Match` weakly, else `If-Modified-Since`, GET and HEAD only), and `Responder::notModified($cache)` answers 304 without rendering. `Responder` takes the `Release` as a new optional last argument, and the kernel binds an unnamed one for an app to replace with its own.

## Core

- `Versions::commit()` is the commit the checkout is on, read from `.git`'s files rather than by running `git`, so it is cheap enough to ask on every request: branches, packed refs, a detached HEAD and worktrees all answer; no repository or no commits is null. The skeleton uses it as the release mixed into every ETag when `APP_RELEASE` isn't set.
