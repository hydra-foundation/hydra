<p>0.9.2 put jobs that ran out of tries in <code>failed_jobs</code> and offered nothing to read them with. The first real use found the gap: a skeleton missing <code>MAIL_FROM_ADDRESS</code> sent its reset mail there, and nothing outside the log said so. Two console commands close it. They and the methods behind them are new, and nothing existing changed, so this is a patch.</p>

<h3>Upgrading</h3>

<p>Nothing is required. To have the commands, register <code>QueueFailedCommand</code> and <code>QueueRetryCommand</code> in <code>bin/console</code>, each given the <code>DatabaseQueue</code>. The skeleton does.</p>

<h3>queue:failed and queue:retry</h3>

<p><code>queue:failed</code> lists failed jobs newest first, with the exception&rsquo;s class and message. <code>queue:retry &lt;id&gt;</code> puts one back on the queue, due now and with its tries restored, and <code>queue:retry --all</code> puts back every one. They call <code>DatabaseQueue::failed()</code> and <code>retry()</code>, which are public for anything else that wants them. A retry runs in a transaction and only adds the job if the failed row was still there to delete, so two retries of the same job add it once. <a href="/docs/queue.html#failed">Failed jobs</a> has the details.</p>

<h3>In the skeleton</h3>

<p>The skeleton&rsquo;s own mail is queued. The password reset and verification controllers push <code>SendPasswordResetLink</code> and <code>SendVerificationLink</code>; the jobs look the user up, make the link and send it. That closes the timing gap on <code>/forgot-password</code>, which took visibly longer for an address that exists: now both answers push a job and return. A mail that fails is retried, then kept in <code>failed_jobs</code>, instead of being logged and lost. The development stack gains Mailpit, which catches SMTP on port 1025 and shows it on 8025; <code>.env.example</code> has the settings. And both commands are registered.</p>
